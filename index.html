GxP Fundamentals — Start Here

Before diving into GAMP, you need to understand the regulatory world these documents live in. This is the foundation every CSV engineer must know.

Beginner Explainer — What is GxP?

GxP stands for "Good x Practice" — a family of regulations that govern how pharmaceutical, medical device, and biotech companies must operate to protect patient safety and product quality. The "x" changes depending on the activity:

  • GMP — Good Manufacturing Practice (manufacturing drugs/devices)
  • GLP — Good Laboratory Practice (non-clinical safety studies)
  • GCP — Good Clinical Practice (clinical trials)
  • GDP — Good Distribution Practice (drug storage/transport)

Any computer system used in these activities must be validated — proven to consistently do what it is designed to do, accurately and reliably. That's where GAMP comes in.

FDA (USA)

Food and Drug Administration. Enforces GxP regulations in the US. Key rules for computer systems: 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records & signatures) and Part 211/820. FDA inspectors can audit your CSV documentation at any time.

EMA / EU (Europe)

European Medicines Agency. For computer systems: EU GMP Annex 11 is the equivalent of 21 CFR Part 11. Also applies to UK (MHRA post-Brexit), Japan (PMDA), and other ICH member countries.

21 CFR Part 11

US FDA rule covering electronic records and electronic signatures. Requires audit trails, access controls, system validation, data integrity protections. Every validated system in pharma must comply with this or Annex 11.

EU GMP Annex 11

EU equivalent of Part 11. Covers computerized systems in GMP environments. Requires validation, data integrity, user management, audit trails, and disaster recovery. Applies across all EU-licensed manufacturers.

The Regulated Industry Ecosystem

Auditor Insight — Why This Matters

In 25+ years of FDA inspections, the first thing an investigator asks is: "Show me your system inventory and validation status." If a system is in GxP scope and not validated per a recognized standard like GAMP, you have a Form 483 observation on day one. GAMP gives you the defensible framework to say: "Here is our documented, risk-based approach."

What is Computer System Validation (CSV)?

CSV is the documented process of ensuring that a computerized system does exactly what it is designed to do — consistently, accurately, and reliably — in a GxP regulated environment. It is not software testing alone; it is a holistic life cycle activity covering planning, requirements, design, testing, release, operation, and retirement.

The V-Model Concept

CSV follows a "V-shaped" model: the left side defines requirements and design; the right side verifies each level through testing. Every specification has a corresponding test protocol.

Key CSV Documents

Validation Plan → User Requirements Spec (URS) → Functional Spec (FS) → Design Spec (DS) → Installation Qualification (IQ) → Operational Qualification (OQ) → Performance Qualification (PQ) → Validation Report.

Risk-Based Approach

Not all systems are equal. GAMP teaches us to focus validation effort on systems with highest impact on patient safety, product quality, and data integrity. Low-risk systems need less documentation.

What is GAMP?

GAMP — Good Automated Manufacturing Practice — is the industry standard for computer system validation in life sciences. Developed by ISPE, it is not a regulation but a globally accepted guidance framework.

About ISPE

The International Society for Pharmaceutical Engineering (ISPE) is a non-profit global organisation of over 19,000 members. It develops technical standards, guides, and training for the pharmaceutical, biotech, and medical device industries.

ISPE publishes the GAMP series of guides — the gold standard for CSV globally. Regulators like the FDA and EMA recognise GAMP as a sound framework, even though it is not law.

GAMP History

  • 1994 — GAMP 1 published (first edition, UK pharma industry)
  • 1998 — GAMP 3 — expanded scope and testing guidance
  • 2001 — GAMP 4 — introduced software categories 1–5
  • 2008 — GAMP 5 First Edition — risk-based approach, V-model
  • 2022 — GAMP 5 Second Edition — agile, blockchain, AI/ML, cloud
  • 2025/26 — GAMP AI Guide — dedicated AI-enabled system guidance
Auditor Insight — GAMP's Legal Status

GAMP is not a regulation — you cannot be cited for "violating GAMP." However, FDA Form 483 observations frequently reference "failure to follow industry-accepted practices." When your validation programme is built on GAMP, you have a defensible, globally recognised methodology. I have seen many 483s disappear when a company could demonstrate a robust GAMP-based programme. Regulators expect you to follow something systematic — GAMP is the most recognised standard in the world for this.

Who Uses GAMP?

Pharmaceutical Companies

Validating LIMS, ERP (SAP), MES, QMS, chromatography data systems (CDS), clinical trial systems, and manufacturing control systems.

Medical Device Companies

Qualifying design control software, production systems, complaint management, and AI-driven diagnostic tools. Also subject to 21 CFR Part 820 / EU MDR.

Biotech / Gene Therapy

Validating bioreactor control systems, cell therapy manufacturing systems, genomics data platforms, and increasingly AI/ML tools for drug discovery.

CROs / CDMOs

Contract research and manufacturing organisations must maintain validated systems for all GxP activities on behalf of their pharma clients. Client audits are frequent.

5 Key Concepts of GAMP 5

Product & Process Understanding

Know your business process deeply before automating it. Understand what GxP-critical functions the system performs. Focus validation effort on what matters most to patient safety.

2

Life Cycle Approach within a QMS

Validation is not a one-time event — it's an ongoing life cycle from concept through retirement, embedded within your Quality Management System (QMS).

3

Scalable Life Cycle Activities

Apply effort proportional to risk. A simple spreadsheet needs less validation than a complex MES. Use critical thinking to determine what's required — avoid "checkbox" validation.

4

Science-Based Quality Risk Management

Use formal QRM (based on ICH Q9) to identify, assess, and control risks to patient safety, product quality, and data integrity. Document your risk decisions.

5

Leveraging Supplier Involvement

Suppliers (software vendors) have knowledge of their products. Use their testing, documentation, and SDLCs to reduce your validation burden — but accountability stays with you.

Beginner Explainer — Why a Separate AI Guide?

Traditional GAMP 5 was written for deterministic software — code that behaves the same way every time given the same input. AI systems are different: they learn from data, their behaviour can change over time (model drift), they can be biased, and their decision-making can be difficult to explain. These unique properties require additional guidance beyond GAMP 5 alone.

AI Terminology Explained Simply

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Technology that enables computers to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence — pattern recognition, decision-making, language understanding. Umbrella term covering all the below.

Machine Learning (ML)

AI systems that learn from data rather than following explicit rules. Given enough examples (training data), an ML model finds patterns and makes predictions. Unlike traditional software, ML models can improve — or degrade — over time.

Neural Networks / Deep Learning

ML models inspired by the brain. Multiple layers of "neurons" process data to recognise complex patterns. Used in image analysis (e.g., detecting defects in tablets), text analysis, and genomics.

Large Language Models (LLMs)

AI trained on vast amounts of text to generate human-like responses. Examples: GPT-4, Claude. In pharma, used for document drafting, literature review. LLMs with Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) can answer questions from your own document library.

Generative AI

AI that creates new content — text, images, molecules, code. In drug discovery, generative AI proposes new molecular structures. In manufacturing, it can synthesize test data. Requires special attention to data integrity and hallucination risk.

Model Drift

When real-world data changes over time, an AI model's performance degrades — it "drifts." In GxP, this is critical: a model that performed well at validation may give unreliable outputs a year later without retraining. Requires ongoing monitoring.

Key Difference: Iterative Loops in AI

Unlike traditional GAMP 5 which follows a mostly linear lifecycle, AI systems require iterative loops: a model may need retraining due to drift, new data may require re-validation, and the project phase may cycle multiple times before a stable production model is achieved. GAMP AI Guide explicitly accommodates this iterative nature.

Auditor Alert — EU AI Act Compliance Gap

The EU AI Act is binding regulation, unlike GAMP. From August 2026, high-risk AI systems in healthcare must be registered in the EU AI database and undergo conformity assessment. This is entirely new territory for most pharmaceutical validation teams. The GAMP AI Guide is currently the only industry-specific guidance that bridges GAMP validation methodology with EU AI Act obligations. If your company uses AI in any GxP decision-making process in Europe, this is an immediate compliance priority.

10 Key Differences — Deep Dive

Understanding these differences is essential for any CSV engineer working in a company that is or plans to be using AI. Each difference has practical implications for your validation programme.

1 Scope: All Systems vs AI-Specific
GAMP 5

Covers all GxP computerized systems regardless of technology. LIMS, ERP, MES, CDS, spreadsheets, lab instruments, process control systems — everything uses the same GAMP 5 framework. Cat 1–5 provides a single taxonomy.

AI Guide

Explicitly scoped to AI-enabled systems only. It is designed to be used in parallel with GAMP 5, not to replace it. For an AI system, you need both: GAMP 5 for the overall framework and the AI Guide for AI-specific activities.

Auditor Insight

I am already seeing FDA inspectors ask: "How does your AI validation programme extend your existing GAMP-based CSV programme?" Companies that try to apply GAMP 5 alone to AI systems often miss model governance, drift monitoring, and bias assessment. Both documents must be in your SOP framework for AI systems.

2 Life Cycle: Linear vs Iterative

GAMP 5's lifecycle is structured but flexible — it supports both waterfall and agile. AI systems require a fundamentally iterative approach because models must be retrained as data and performance change.

GAMP 5 LIFECYCLE

Concept → Project (Plan/Spec/Build/Test/Report) → Operation → Retirement. Change triggers re-validation as needed. Primarily forward-moving with controlled change loops.

AI GUIDE LIFECYCLE

Same phases but with explicit retraining loops: Operation phase may trigger back to Project phase when drift is detected. Model versioning creates multiple validated states. Iterative experimentation is a defined activity.

3 Data: Integrity Focus vs Full Data Governance

Both documents care about data — but at very different levels of depth.

  • GAMP 5: Focuses on data integrity (ALCOA+ principles — Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate), audit trails, and record management. Primarily concerned with output data produced by systems.
  • AI Guide: Adds a full data governance framework (Appendix M7) covering training data quality, data lineage, data provenance, data preparation controls, feature engineering governance, data access controls, and ongoing data operations. Training data is treated as a first-class GxP artefact.
  • Key insight: In AI, a garbage-in-garbage-out failure is a GxP risk. If training data is biased, incomplete, or not representative of the intended use population, the model will make poor predictions — potentially affecting patient safety. GAMP 5 alone doesn't address this.

5. Regulatory — EU AI Act

GAMP 5 references EU GMP and FDA. The AI Guide is the first GAMP document to explicitly integrate the EU AI Act (2024), requiring organisations to classify AI systems by risk level and implement conformity assessments for high-risk AI. This creates an entirely new compliance obligation on top of traditional GxP.

6. Supplier Types — AI Model Providers

GAMP 5 supplier management focuses on software vendors (SAP, LIMS vendors). The AI Guide adds: pre-trained model suppliers (cloud AI APIs like Azure AI, Google Vertex), open-source model providers (Hugging Face models), and data suppliers. Each requires a different assessment approach.

7. Testing — Performance Metrics vs Pass/Fail

GAMP 5 testing is binary: tests pass or fail against defined expected results. AI model "testing" uses statistical performance metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, AUC-ROC, F1 score). The AI Guide introduces the concept of acceptable performance thresholds as the AI equivalent of acceptance criteria.

8. Appendix Structure Redesign

GAMP 5 separates Development appendices (D-series) and Operation appendices (O-series). The AI Guide restructures these into Phase appendices (P1–P4), each covering both development and operation aspects of that phase holistically. This better reflects the iterative nature of AI where boundaries between phases blur.

9. Human Oversight — New Requirement

GAMP 5 requires human-approved change control but assumes the system output is deterministic. The AI Guide explicitly addresses human oversight as a safety mechanism: for high-risk AI decisions, a qualified human must review the AI output before acting on it. This is both a GAMP AI Guide requirement and an EU AI Act mandate.

10. Trustworthy AI — New Concept Category

No equivalent in GAMP 5. Trustworthy AI requires systems to be: explainable (can explain why), fair/unbiased (equitable across populations), robust (stable under perturbation), privacy-preserving, and safe. These are not testable with traditional IQ/OQ/PQ protocols — new methods are required.

The Auditor's Perspective

From 25+ years of FDA and notified body inspections across pharmaceutical, biotech, and medical device companies — here is what inspectors look for when they audit your CSV and AI validation programme.

The Golden Rule of GxP Auditing

"If it isn't documented, it didn't happen." This is not a cliché — it is the operating principle of every FDA investigator. A brilliant validation programme with poor documentation will fail an audit. A mediocre programme with excellent, consistent, complete documentation will survive. GAMP gives you the framework; your SOPs and records are what the inspector actually sees.

Most Common FDA 483 Observations Related to Computerized Systems

No Written Validation Plan

System in GxP use with no documented validation plan. "The firm failed to establish a documented validation plan..." — Seen in majority of 483s for computerized systems.

Audit Trail Disabled / Not Reviewed

Audit trail is a fundamental 21 CFR Part 11 requirement. Disabled audit trails or not conducting regular audit trail reviews are among the top 5 most cited findings in FDA drug GMP inspections.

Untested Changes

"Software version X was installed without impact assessment or re-qualification." A patch applied without change control and re-testing is a critical observation. Every change must be evaluated.

No Traceability

Test protocols that cannot be traced back to specific user requirements. "We cannot determine which tests cover which requirements" — the traceability matrix is missing or incomplete.

Inadequate Supplier Control

Using COTS software without any supplier assessment or quality agreement. Outsourcing does not outsource accountability — you must assess and monitor your software suppliers.

Spreadsheet Validation Absent

Regulated calculations performed in Excel spreadsheets without any validation. Spreadsheets used for GxP decisions must be validated per GAMP S3 guidance. Extremely common finding.

Glossary of Key Terms

Essential terminology from GAMP 5 and the GAMP AI Guide. Every CSV engineer must know these terms.

ALCOA+
Data integrity principles: Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate. Plus: Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available. Foundation of data integrity in GxP.
AI-Enabled Computerized System
A computerized system that incorporates one or more AI systems. The highest level of the GAMP AI Guide system hierarchy.
Algorithmic Bias
When an AI model produces systematically unfair or inaccurate outputs for certain subgroups (e.g., by race, gender, age). A GxP risk in clinical AI applications.
Audit Trail
A secure, computer-generated, time-stamped record that allows reconstruction of a sequence of events relating to the creation, modification, or deletion of an electronic record. Required by 21 CFR Part 11 and Annex 11.
CAPA
Corrective and Preventive Action. A process to investigate deviations, identify root causes, and implement corrections (fix the current problem) and preventions (stop it happening again).
Computerized System Validation (CSV)
The documented process of demonstrating that a computerized system consistently performs its intended function, accurately and reliably, in a GxP regulated environment.
Context of Use (CoU)
The specific conditions under which an AI model is applied, including the intended patient population, clinical setting, user type, and decision supported. Critical for AI scope definition.
EU AI Act
The European Union's binding regulation on artificial intelligence (2024). Classifies AI systems by risk level and imposes conformity assessment requirements on high-risk AI in safety-critical sectors including healthcare.
FMEA
Failure Mode and Effects Analysis. A systematic risk assessment tool that identifies potential failure modes, their effects, and their causes. Used in GAMP QRM process.
GAMP
Good Automated Manufacturing Practice. Industry guidance published by ISPE for computer system validation in GxP regulated industries.
GxP
Good "x" Practice — umbrella term for Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP), Good Laboratory Practice (GLP), Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Good Distribution Practice (GDP), and related regulations.
ICH Q9
International Council for Harmonisation guideline on Quality Risk Management. The scientific basis for GAMP's risk-based approach. Defines the QRM process used throughout GAMP 5.
IQ / OQ / PQ
Installation Qualification / Operational Qualification / Performance Qualification. The three phases of system testing in the V-model: IQ verifies installation, OQ verifies functions, PQ verifies fitness for intended use.
ISPE
International Society for Pharmaceutical Engineering. The global non-profit organisation that publishes the GAMP series of guides and other pharmaceutical engineering standards.
Machine Learning (ML)
A subset of AI in which systems learn patterns from data rather than following explicitly programmed rules. Includes supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning approaches.
Model Drift
Degradation of an AI model's performance over time due to changes in the real-world data distribution compared to training data. Requires ongoing monitoring and potential retraining.
Model Governance
The policies, processes, and controls for managing AI models throughout their lifecycle: development, deployment, monitoring, versioning, retraining, and retirement. Introduced in GAMP AI Guide M7.
Periodic Review
A scheduled, documented assessment of whether a validated system remains in a state of control and continues to meet its intended purpose. Required throughout the operational life of a GxP system.
Quality by Design (QbD)
A systematic approach that embeds quality into the design and development process from the outset, rather than relying on testing to catch quality problems after the fact.
Quality Management System (QMS)
The management system that directs and controls an organisation with respect to quality. The GAMP life cycle operates within the broader QMS framework.
Quality Risk Management (QRM)
A systematic process for the identification, assessment, control, communication, and review of risks to patient safety, product quality, and data integrity.
Traceability Matrix
A document that maps and tracks user requirements to design specifications and test cases. Ensures every requirement is tested and every test can be justified by a requirement.
Trustworthy AI
AI that is explainable, fair/unbiased, robust, privacy-preserving, and safe. A key concept introduced in the GAMP AI Guide, aligned with EU AI Act principles.
URS (User Requirements Specification)
A document that describes what a system must do from the user's perspective. The top level of the GAMP specification hierarchy. All other specs and tests must trace back to the URS.
Validation Master Plan (VMP)
A high-level document describing the overall validation strategy for an organisation or site. Covers philosophy, scope, organisational structure, and references to individual validation plans.
21 CFR Part 11
US FDA regulation governing electronic records and electronic signatures in pharmaceutical, biotech, and medical device companies. Sets requirements for audit trails, access control, system validation, and record integrity.
